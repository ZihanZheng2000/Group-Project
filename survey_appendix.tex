\textbf{Survey on Settling Shared Expenses with Friends}

\emph{Needfinding survey English version}

\subsubsection*{Before you begin}

Please think of \textbf{one or more times in the past year} when you and friends took part in an activity and needed to settle shared costs afterward. For each experience you draw on, \textbf{at least 3 people in total} shared \textbf{at least two expenses}, and at least one person paid upfront. You can include experiences involving Splitwise, another tool, a group chat, or just conversation. \textbf{Different questions may draw on different qualifying experiences. In the questions below, ``these experiences'' means the qualifying experiences you can remember. Select everything you remember happening; you do not need to guess about experiences you cannot recall.} When a question asks for one example, choose one you remember clearly.

We will not ask for names, account details, or exact amounts. All questions are multiple-choice except the last two, which are optional written answers. For questions marked Select all that apply, you may choose more than one answer. An answer marked Select alone must not be combined with other answers. Choose I cannot remember alone only when you cannot recall enough to select any other answer to that question.

\textbf{Q14:} Seven people entered an answer. Three described a specific difficulty: too little expense detail, uncertainty about splitting a cost among group members, or the work of entering an amount and category by hand. Four entries were "none," "not applicable," or similar. The comment about manual entry was one reason the team compared item-by-item entry with showing the details during settlement.

\textbf{Q15:} Seven people entered an answer. Two described a specific difficulty: recalculating tax from a bank transaction after losing a restaurant receipt, and the work of joining an expense-sharing group. Five entries were "none," "not applicable," or similar. The receipt example relates to checking an amount. The group-joining example sits near the edge of our task and may matter if it gets in the way of settling up.

This summary uses totals for each option. Respondent-level responses would let us look more closely at links between questions and at the experiences of people who used different tools.
